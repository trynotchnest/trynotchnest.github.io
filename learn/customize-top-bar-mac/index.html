How to Customize the Top Bar on Mac

Your Mac's top bar is more flexible than it looks. Here's how to customize the menu bar and the notch — natively, and with one app that does the heavy lifting.

Updated June 15, 20266 min readBy Satnam Singh

The "top bar" on a Mac is two things: the menu bar that runs across the top of the screen, and the notch that sits in the middle of it on modern MacBooks. Most guides only cover the first. The notch is where the real gains are, because it is the one part of your display that ships doing nothing at all.

This page focuses on the notch half. If you want the menu bar mechanics — Command-dragging icons, Control Center toggles, auto-hide, clock formatting — those are covered in full in how to customise the macOS menu bar.

The two halves of the top bar

On a MacBook Pro (2021 or later) or MacBook Air (M2 or later), the notch physically divides the menu bar. macOS treats the region behind it as unusable space and lays status icons out from the right edge leftward, hiding anything that collides with the cutout.

So the top bar you actually control is:

  • The status area — right of the notch. Reorderable, hideable, entirely yours.
  • The app menus — left of the notch. Fixed by whichever app is frontmost.
  • The notch itself — dead by default, and the subject of the rest of this page.

What Mac notch customization actually means

There are three distinct approaches, and people often conflate them:

  1. Hide it. Make the notch disappear visually, either with a black-topped wallpaper or by running a scaled resolution that letterboxes the menu bar. You lose vertical screen space and gain nothing functional. See how to hide the notch on Mac.
  2. Decorate it. Wallpapers designed to play with the cutout rather than mask it — the notch becomes part of the image. See Mac notch wallpapers.
  3. Use it. Turn the notch into an interactive surface that expands on hover. This is the only option that gives you something back.

Turning the notch into a control surface

Customizing the top bar on a Mac with no notch

On a Mac mini, iMac, Mac Studio, MacBook Air M1 or any pre-2021 MacBook Pro, there is no cutout — but the top-centre region of the display is still empty. NotchNest renders there as a slim drop-down panel anchored to the top of the screen, with every widget behaving the same way. You get the same customization surface without the hardware.

Tune spacing and density

Menu bar spacing is fixed by macOS and cannot be changed. Perceived clutter can. The combination that works: cut always-on icons down to the four or five you genuinely watch, push the rest behind an organiser, and move the things you interact with — timer, clipboard, agenda — into the notch panel where they get room to breathe.

Try the interactive notch playground to preview the layout before installing anything.

Reverting any of it

Every change described here is reversible in seconds and none of it touches system files. Icon order resets by Command-dragging back, or by relaunching the owning app. Control Center toggles flip back. Notch widgets turn off individually, and uninstalling the app returns the notch to being dead space.

Frequently asked

What is Mac notch customization?
It's repurposing the notch area from empty space into a usable control surface. Apps like NotchNest let you choose which widgets appear there and how they behave on hover. It's distinct from hiding the notch, which removes it visually but gives you nothing in return.
Can you customize the Mac top bar without third-party apps?
The menu bar half, largely yes — reorder icons with Command-drag, remove them, and toggle system icons in System Settings → Control Center. The notch half, no. macOS exposes no setting for putting content in the notch, so that part requires an app.
Why do my menu bar icons hide behind the notch?
macOS lays status icons out from the right edge leftward. When they reach the notch it hides the overflow rather than wrapping around it, so the leftmost icons vanish. Removing or grouping icons fixes it — see menu bar icons missing behind the notch.
Does customizing the top bar affect performance?
Reordering and hiding icons costs nothing. A notch app costs whatever that app costs — a native SwiftUI one that sleeps when the panel is closed is negligible; anything polling on a short interval is worth checking in Activity Monitor.
Is customizing the top bar reversible?
Yes. Every change — icon order, visibility, notch widgets — is reversible in seconds with no system files touched.
