How to customise the macOS menu bar

A practical guide to reordering, hiding and extending the macOS menu bar — using built-in settings, free third-party apps, and the notch.

Updated September 8, 202611 min readBy Satnam Singh

The macOS menu bar is the strip running across the top of your display. It has been part of the Mac since 1984 and it is still one of the few pieces of macOS you can completely reshape without opening Terminal.

What the macOS menu bar is made of

Before changing anything, it helps to know that the bar is really three zones, and each one obeys different rules:

  • Left — the Apple menu and app menus. The menu never moves. Everything to its right (File, Edit, View…) belongs to whichever app is frontmost, and changes as you switch apps. You cannot reorder or hide these.
  • Right — status items. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Battery, Sound, Focus, plus every third-party app that puts an icon up there. This is the zone you can actually rearrange.
  • Far right — Control Center and the clock. Both are pinned. They cannot be dragged, though the clock's format is configurable and Control Center's contents are not.

On a MacBook Pro (2021 or later) or MacBook Air (M2 or later) there is a fourth zone: the notch sits in the middle of the bar and splits it in two. macOS treats the space behind it as unusable, which is why crowded menu bars start losing icons on those machines.

Almost every menu bar setting lives in one place: System Settings → Control Center. Keep that panel open while you work through the steps below.

Here's how to customise every part of it, in order of effort.

1. Reorder icons (free, 10 seconds)

Hold Command and drag any menu bar icon left or right. That's the whole trick. It works on Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Battery, Sound, Now Playing, AirDrop, Focus and third-party apps — everything except the clock and Control Center, which are pinned to the far right. The order survives reboots.

Full walkthrough, including how to rescue an icon you dragged into the void and how ordering behaves around the notch: How to move menu bar icons on Mac.

2. Hide or show built-in icons

Open System Settings → Control Center. Every module has three options: Show in Menu Bar, Don't Show in Menu Bar, Only Show When Active (where applicable).

Worth knowing:

  • Battery can show a percentage by toggling "Show Percentage".
  • Now Playing can be set to appear only when music or video plays.
  • Spotlight can be hidden — Command-Space still works.
  • Clock can show the date, day of week, seconds, AM/PM under "Clock Options".

3. Hide third-party icons

Same Command-drag trick: drag an icon down off the menu bar and it disappears. The app itself keeps running; you just lose the icon. To bring it back, open the app's preferences and re-enable the menu bar icon there.

4. Menu bar managers (for dense menu bars)

Past roughly ten icons, macOS alone stops being enough and an organiser app pays for itself. Ice is open source and free; it hides icons behind a chevron and lets you split "always visible" from "click to reveal". Bartender is paid and more polished, adding triggers, hotkeys and search.

On a 14" MacBook Pro an organiser does double duty, because it stops icons disappearing into the notch once the bar fills up. For the wider field — clipboard managers, system monitors, timers — see the best Mac menu bar apps.

5. Use the notch as a second menu bar

If your Mac has a notch — every MacBook Pro from 2021 onwards and every MacBook Air M2 onwards — you have a whole second region you can make interactive. macOS itself doesn't do anything with the notch, but NotchNest wraps it in a hover-activated glass panel.

Hover the notch with the cursor and the panel drops down: calendar, AI clipboard, Quick Notes, Pomodoro timer, Spotify/Apple Music controls, drag-to-AirDrop, File-Tray for recent files, and a one-tap camera mirror. Every widget can be toggled on or off in Settings, so you only see what you use.

That's a genuine menu-bar extension. You haven't added another icon to a crowded top bar — you've put functionality in space that was sitting empty.

6. Style the clock

System Settings → Control Center → Clock Options. You can show:

  • Digital or analog clock
  • Day of the week
  • Date
  • Seconds
  • AM/PM or 24-hour

Power users often hide the menu bar clock entirely and put one in the notch via NotchNest, giving a larger, more legible clock with built-in calendar awareness.

7. Auto-hide the menu bar

System Settings → Control Center → Automatically hide and show the menu bar. Set to "Always", "On Desktop Only", or "In Full Screen Only". Useful on a 13" Air where every vertical pixel counts.

The hidden menu bar reappears when you move the cursor to the top of the screen.

8. Reset everything

If you've broken the menu bar layout and can't remember what was where, quit each app whose icon appears and relaunch it; default position returns. For Apple icons, set them all to "Show in Menu Bar" and the original left-to-right order returns.

9. Menu bars on external displays

Each display gets its own menu bar, but only the active one shows app menus — the others show a dimmed copy. Two settings decide the behaviour:

  • System Settings → Desktop & Dock → "Displays have separate Spaces". On (the default), every screen carries a full menu bar. Off, only the display marked as primary has one, and the others are pure canvas.
  • System Settings → Displays → Arrange. Drag the white bar onto whichever screen should be primary. That's the screen your menu bar and Dock call home.

A detail that surprises people: your icon order is global, not per-display. Rearranging on the laptop screen rearranges on the external monitor too. If your MacBook is clamshelled to a large display, the notch stops being a factor entirely, which is often the fastest fix for a bar that keeps swallowing icons.

10. What changed in macOS Tahoe

macOS Tahoe (macOS 26) rebuilt the menu bar's appearance around Liquid Glass. The bar is now fully transparent by default, picking up colour from the wallpaper behind it rather than sitting on a tinted strip. Two things follow from that:

  • Icon legibility now depends on your wallpaper. A busy or light image makes monochrome status icons hard to read — System Settings → Accessibility → Display → Reduce Transparency puts the solid backing back.
  • The notch is visually more prominent, because there is no longer a dark bar blending into it. Some people counter this with a black-topped wallpaper; others lean in and put widgets there instead.

Everything else in this guide — Command-drag, Control Center toggles, auto-hide — works identically on Sonoma, Sequoia and Tahoe. More detail: macOS Tahoe and the notch.

11. When icons go missing

Three failure modes account for nearly every "my menu bar icon vanished" report:

  1. Swallowed by the notch. The bar ran out of room and macOS parked the overflow behind the cutout. Icons are still there, just unreachable. Four ways to get them back.
  2. Dragged off accidentally. A stray Command-drag downward removes an icon. Re-enable it from inside that app's own preferences — macOS has no undo for this.
  3. Turned off in Control Center. Apple icons set to "Don't Show in Menu Bar" look identical to a bug. Check the Control Center panel first.

Frequently asked

How do I reorder menu bar icons on Mac?

Hold Command and drag the icon left or right. It works for both Apple and third-party status icons. The clock and Control Center are pinned to the far right and cannot be moved.

How do I hide menu bar icons on Mac?

For Apple icons, open System Settings → Control Center and set the module to "Don't Show in Menu Bar". For third-party icons, Command-drag the icon down off the bar, or turn it off inside that app's own preferences. To hide many at once, use an organiser like Ice or Bartender.

Can I customise the macOS menu bar without third-party apps?

Yes. Reordering, hiding built-in icons, clock formatting and auto-hide are all built into macOS. You only need a third-party app when you want to collapse a large number of icons behind a chevron, or add functionality the menu bar does not have.

Do menu bar apps slow down my Mac?

Well-built ones do not. A status item that sleeps until clicked costs essentially nothing. Apps that poll constantly — network monitors, weather widgets on short refresh intervals — are the ones worth watching in Activity Monitor.

Why do my menu bar icons disappear behind the notch?

macOS lays icons out from the right edge leftward. When they reach the notch it hides the overflow rather than wrapping around it. Reduce the number of visible icons, use an organiser, or lower the display resolution one step. Full options: menu bar icons missing behind the notch.

Can I change the menu bar colour?

Not directly. The bar takes its appearance from your wallpaper and your Light/Dark mode setting. On macOS Tahoe it is transparent by default; Accessibility → Display → Reduce Transparency gives you a solid backing instead. A dark wallpaper is the usual way to get a dark bar in Light mode.
